# Preface

The past is never neutral in the present. Every generation inherits a usable history: a set of names, dates, maps, villains, heroes, founding myths, and silences. Some parts are true. Some are partial. Some are useful because they are partial.

This book starts from a simple premise: important historical thinking often begins when the familiar story stops feeling complete.

Alternative history, as used here, is not a retreat from evidence. It is a demand for better reading: stronger sources, sharper questions, wider archives, and a clearer sense of who benefits when a narrative becomes stable.

The center of the project is theory and criticism. The book grows from a deliberate collection of texts, not from broad scraping, topic crawling, or automated harvesting. A source belongs here when it changes how readers understand interpretation, memory, power, evidence, authorship, translation, form, or historical narration.

The Norton anthology matters to this first phase because it gives the project a recognizable theory-and-criticism spine. It lets us work through major problems higher education has inherited: rhetoric and civic speech, poetry and judgment, interpretation and authority, education and discipline, commentary and transmission, beauty and knowledge, public reason and institutional power. The goal is not to accept Norton as the final boundary of the project. The goal is to use it carefully as a structured starting point, then ask what it includes, what it omits, and what other texts must enter the conversation.

The working version of the project is also a private knowledge base. It is meant to behave more like a wiki or an Obsidian graph than a finished linear book: a place where Gorgias can sit near Plato, Aristotle, Quintilian, and later critics; where a passage can become a node; where a question can connect works that would otherwise remain in separate boxes.

The task is not simply to collect great texts. It is to make careful relationships between them.

## Commitments

- Evidence matters. Every claim should point toward a source, archive, or named interpretive tradition.
- Selection matters. Every included text should have a clear reason for belonging to this collection.
- Fidelity matters. The project should try to be true to the text before asking what the text can do for the project.
- Perspective matters. The same event can look different from a palace, plantation, ship, kitchen, border, prison, or classroom.
- Absence matters. Missing records are historical facts too, especially when power decided what could be recorded.
- Imagination matters. Counterfactual thought can reveal contingency, but it should not pretend to be proof.
- Language matters. The book should avoid sensationalism and keep a clear line between documentation, interpretation, and speculation.

## How To Use This Project

Start with a thinker, a text, a passage, or a question. Follow the links outward. A strong page should show why a source matters, where it sits in the collection, which other texts press on it, and what still needs to be checked.

One important use case is higher education itself. The project should help students, teachers, researchers, and institutional citizens ask what education is for: civic formation, critical judgment, disciplined attention, public responsibility, work, freedom, memory, or something else. Those questions should stay visible when we read ancient rhetoric, medieval pedagogy, modern criticism, colonial archives, labor history, political theory, and contemporary institutional life.

The private workspace is allowed to be provisional. It can hold rough notes, restricted-source audit trails, review flags, and incomplete constellations. Public-facing claims should be slower: cited, rights-aware, and clearly separated from private extraction work.

## What This Book Is Not

It is not a list of "hidden truths." It is not a collection of unsupported claims. It is not a replacement for academic history. It is also not a bulk archive, a database scrape, or a pile of available PDFs.

It is a source-forward companion for readers who want history to become strange again in the best sense: less automatic, less obedient, and more alive to possibility.
