# Preface

The past is never neutral in the present. Every generation inherits a usable history: a set of names, dates, maps, villains, heroes, founding myths, and silences. Some parts are true. Some are partial. Some are useful because they are partial.

This book starts from a simple premise: the most important historical work often begins when the familiar story stops feeling complete.

Alternative history, as used here, is not a retreat from evidence. It is a demand for better reading: stronger sources, sharper questions, wider archives, and a clearer sense of who benefits from a stable narrative.

The center of the project is theory and criticism. The book will grow from a deliberate collection of texts, not from broad scraping or automated harvesting. A source belongs here when it changes how readers understand interpretation, memory, power, evidence, authorship, translation, or historical narration.

## Commitments

- Evidence matters. Every claim should point toward a source, archive, or named interpretive tradition.
- Selection matters. Every included text should have a clear reason for belonging to this collection.
- Perspective matters. The same event can look different from a palace, plantation, ship, kitchen, border, prison, or classroom.
- Absence matters. Missing records are historical facts too, especially when power decided what could be recorded.
- Imagination matters. Counterfactual thought can reveal contingency, but it should not pretend to be proof.
- Language matters. The book should avoid sensationalism and keep a clear line between documentation, interpretation, and speculation.

## What This Book Is Not

It is not a list of "hidden truths." It is not a collection of unsupported claims. It is not a replacement for academic history. It is also not a bulk archive, a database scrape, or a pile of available PDFs. It is a public, readable, source-forward companion for people who want history to become strange again in the best sense: less automatic, less obedient, and more alive to possibility.
