# Erased Archives

The archive is often treated as a neutral storehouse. It is better understood as a battlefield of survival.

Records can be burned, classified, mistranslated, purchased, buried, ignored, or never created. People can be made illegible by law, language, race, gender, caste, class, empire, or the ordinary violence of bureaucracy.

## Questions For This Chapter

- Who had the power to create records?
- Who had the power to preserve them?
- What forms of memory survived outside official archives?
- What happens when oral history, song, ritual, craft, land, and family memory are treated as historical evidence?
- How should a public book write about absence without filling it with fantasy?

## Candidate Topics

- Slave narratives and the politics of testimony.
- Indigenous histories and settler archives.

- Colonial maps as instruments of possession.
- Lost, destroyed, or inaccessible archives.
- Translation as historical power.
