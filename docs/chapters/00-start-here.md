# Start Here

This chapter is the working map for the book. The project should feel like a curated reader, not a general archive.

## Proposed Shape

1. Theory and criticism: the core texts that teach readers how history is narrated and judged.
2. Methods: how to read against inherited narratives.
3. Rupture points: events where the story changes depending on who tells it.
4. Erased archives: what survives, what disappears, and why.
5. Counterfactual method: disciplined speculation as historical inquiry.
6. Memory and myth: monuments, textbooks, maps, anniversaries, and national stories.
7. Lives against the frame: authors and witnesses whose work alters the archive.
8. Futures of the past: how historical imagination changes present action.

## Entry Types

- Thinker profile: a person whose work reframes historical understanding.
- Text constellation: a curated group of texts that argue, echo, resist, inherit, or transform one another.
- Theory note: a compact guide to a concept, argument, or critical vocabulary.
- Historical problem dossier: a contested or misremembered historical problem.
- Excerpt entry: a short source passage with commentary and rights status.
- Narrative essay: an original chapter section connecting multiple sources.
- Reading path: a curated sequence for readers new to a theme.

## Drafting Rule

Each piece should earn its place by changing the reader's relationship to a familiar story, a critical concept, or a method of reading.

## First Experience

The first public experience should not be a database. It should feel like entering a room where major texts are already in conversation. Readers should be able to follow a question across several thinkers and see how each text changes the others.
