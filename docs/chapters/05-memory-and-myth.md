# Memory and Myth

Public history lives in monuments, textbooks, museums, holidays, maps, family stories, names of streets, and the way a nation introduces itself to children.

A myth is not simply a falsehood. It is a story with social power. Some myths preserve real experience. Some myths protect domination. Some myths make violence feel like destiny.

## Questions For This Chapter

- Which historical stories are repeated as origin stories?
- Who is asked to identify with those stories?
- What must be forgotten for the story to remain stable?
- How do monuments and anniversaries turn interpretation into landscape?
- What does it mean to inherit a past without obeying it?
