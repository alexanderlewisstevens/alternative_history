# Rupture Points

Some events become hinges in public memory. They are repeated so often that their interpretation feels natural. A rupture point is an event where changing perspective changes the whole story.

## Candidate Dossiers

| Event | Conventional Frame | Alternative Questions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1492 and the Atlantic world | Discovery and expansion | Whose world ended, whose world began, and whose archive named the event? |
| The Haitian Revolution | Slave revolt at the edge of empire | What happens to modern freedom narratives when enslaved people become central political theorists? |
| The Industrial Revolution | Innovation and progress | What forms of labor, enclosure, extraction, and empire made the machine age possible? |
| Reconstruction in the United States | Failed postwar settlement | What if Reconstruction is read as a democratic revolution overthrown? |
| Partition and decolonization | Nation-making | How do borders become historical trauma, memory, and administrative fact? |

Each dossier should include primary sources, later interpretations, a rights note, and a "what this changes" section.
